% ====================== ABSTRACT ======================
\section{Abstract}
EasySeva is a responsive web portal that helps ordinary citizens find, apply for,
and track government services in a Tamil Nadu setting. People can use it in their
own language and move at their own pace. We built it as a Human Computer
Interaction project, and we designed it mainly for first time users and for people
who are not very comfortable with technology. These are exactly the users who
often find official portals confusing, full of jargon, and easy to get wrong.

This report explains what the application does, why it is useful, and how we
applied well known HCI principles on every screen. We look at the interface
through four lenses that complement each other. These are Jakob Nielsen's ten
usability heuristics, Ben Shneiderman's eight golden rules, Don Norman's
principles of interaction design, and the WCAG accessibility pillars known as
POUR. For each page of the application we show a screenshot, describe what the page
is for, and list the principles we used. For every principle we say what it means
and how it shows up on that screen. The report ends with a coverage table and a
short reflection on how good design helps real people have a better experience.

% ====================== INTRODUCTION ======================
\section{Introduction}

\subsection{What is EasySeva}
Applying for a government document is something almost every family has to do at
some point. It could be an income certificate, a scholarship, a ration card, or an
old age pension. Yet the online experience is usually hard. Forms often put thirty
fields on a single page, use acronyms that are never explained, work only in
English, give you no way to save your progress, and report status in words that
only an official would understand. For a first time user, or for someone who is
not confident with computers, all of this becomes a wall.

EasySeva rethinks that experience around the way citizens actually think, rather
than the way departments are organised. It offers the following.
\begin{itemize}
  \item A catalogue of nine services across six citizen friendly categories.
        These are Certificates, Benefits, Education, Family, Senior Citizen, and
        Document Updates.
  \item A guided flow that moves through an eligibility check, a document
        checklist, a short form broken into steps, a review, and a confirmation.
  \item Passwordless login using a one time code, the ability to save a draft and
        come back later, and tracking by reference number.
  \item A trilingual interface in English, Tamil, and Hindi, along with built in
        accessibility controls for larger text, higher contrast, and reduced
        motion.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Who is it for and why is it useful}
The people we designed for are the ones who usually feel uneasy with bureaucracy.
They include first time applicants, elderly users, and people with limited English
or limited digital experience. EasySeva is useful because it lowers every barrier
that normally causes a person to give up or make a mistake. It speaks plain
language, it shows exactly what is needed before the user starts, it checks the
input as it is typed, it never throws away the user's work, and it explains every
status in human terms. The result is that a citizen can complete an application
correctly on the first try, and can do so with confidence and dignity.

\subsection{How HCI enables a good interaction}
Human Computer Interaction is about designing systems around what people can do,
what they struggle with, and what they expect. A government portal is a very good
example of a task where this matters a lot. The task is high stakes, it happens
rarely, and it tends to make people anxious. The user may touch the system only
once a year, cannot afford a mistake, and has had no training. When we apply ideas
that are well understood in this field, such as keeping status visible, preventing
errors, favouring recognition over memory, giving the user control, and designing
for everyone, the whole experience changes. An anxious and error prone chore
becomes calm, predictable, and forgiving. The rest of this report shows, screen by
screen, how we did that.

% ====================== FRAMEWORKS ======================
\section{HCI Principles and Frameworks Used}
This section defines the frameworks we refer to in the page by page analysis.

\subsection{Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics}
\begin{enumerate}[label=N\arabic*.]
  \item \textbf{Visibility of system status.} Keep users informed with timely and
        appropriate feedback.
  \item \textbf{Match between the system and the real world.} Speak the users'
        language with familiar words and ideas.
  \item \textbf{User control and freedom.} Offer clear exits, undo, and the
        freedom to change course.
  \item \textbf{Consistency and standards.} Make similar things look and behave
        in similar ways.
  \item \textbf{Error prevention.} Design so that problems do not happen in the
        first place.
  \item \textbf{Recognition rather than recall.} Keep options and information
        visible so people do not have to remember them.
  \item \textbf{Flexibility and efficiency of use.} Let both new and experienced
        users work in the way that suits them.
  \item \textbf{Aesthetic and minimalist design.} Show only what is relevant and
        remove clutter.
  \item \textbf{Help users recognise, diagnose, and recover from errors.} Explain
        errors in plain language and offer a way forward.
  \item \textbf{Help and documentation.} Provide help that is easy to find and
        focused on the task.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Shneiderman's 8 Golden Rules}
\begin{enumerate}[label=S\arabic*.]
  \item Strive for consistency.
  \item Cater to universal usability, from novices to experts, including
        accessibility.
  \item Offer informative feedback.
  \item Design dialogs to yield closure.
  \item Prevent errors.
  \item Permit easy reversal of actions.
  \item Keep users in control.
  \item Reduce short term memory load.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Norman's Interaction Design Principles}
\begin{enumerate}[label=D\arabic*.]
  \item \textbf{Affordances and signifiers.} Controls look like what they do and
        signal how to use them.
  \item \textbf{Feedback.} Every action produces a result the user can perceive.
  \item \textbf{Mapping.} The link between a control and its effect feels natural,
        for example a progress bar that matches the steps completed.
  \item \textbf{Constraints.} The design limits invalid actions.
  \item \textbf{Consistency.} Similar operations use similar elements.
  \item \textbf{Conceptual model.} The interface helps users build a correct
        picture of how the process works.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{WCAG Accessibility Pillars, known as POUR}
Content should be Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, and Robust. EasySeva
addresses these through contrast aware theming, full keyboard operation with
visible focus, plain language content, and semantic markup that assistive
technology can read.

% ====================== DESIGN FOUNDATIONS ======================
\section{Design System and Foundations}
Before the page analysis, three foundations are worth noting because they support
consistency, universal usability, and accessibility on every screen.

\subsubsection{A consistent visual language}
Every screen is built from a small set of reusable pieces, namely Button, Card,
Input, Alert, Dialog, Badge, and Progress. They all draw on shared design tokens,
including a fixed colour palette, consistent spacing and corner radius, and a
single typeface. Because of this, a primary action, a status badge, or a form
field looks and behaves the same way everywhere. Learning one screen effectively
teaches the user every screen.

\subsubsection{Built in accessibility controls}
Each user can adapt the interface to suit them. There are three text sizes, a high
contrast theme, and a reduced motion mode that respects the operating system
preference. A skip to main content link, visible keyboard focus rings, large touch
targets, and ARIA roles and labels are present throughout.

\subsubsection{A trilingual interface}
The entire interface is available in English, Tamil, and Hindi. The language can be
chosen on the landing page or switched at any time from the header, and the choice
is remembered on the next visit.

\shot{20-accessibility}{The Accessibility Settings page lets each user adjust text

rule to cater to universal usability.}

% ---- Landing ----
\subsection{Landing Page}
\shot{01-landing}{The landing page offers one clear message, an up front language
choice, and two obvious next actions.}
\textbf{Purpose.} Earn trust in a few seconds and send the user to the one thing
they came to do, which is to start a service or track an existing one.
\begin{itemize}
  \prin{Aesthetic and minimalist design (N8)}{Show only what matters}{There is a
    single headline, one supporting line, the language choice, and exactly two
    buttons. Nothing competes for the first time user's attention.}
  \prin{Match with the real world (N2)}{Use the user's language}{The promise that
    government services are made easier, and that you can find, apply, and track
    them in your language and at your pace, is written the way a citizen thinks
    rather than in departmental terms.}
  \prin{Flexibility and efficiency (N7)}{Serve different users}{The language is
    chosen before anything else, so a Tamil or Hindi speaker is never pushed
    through an English wall, as Figure~\ref{fig:tamil} shows.}
  \prin{Recognition rather than recall (N6)}{Make the options visible}{The trust
    strip, which lists secure and private, three languages, save and resume, and
    step by step guidance, surfaces the key capabilities instead of expecting the
    user to discover them.}
  \prin{Affordance and signifiers (D1)}{Controls should look usable}{The solid
    Get Started button clearly invites the main action, while the lighter Track
    button is visually secondary, so importance matches appearance.}
\end{itemize}

% ---- Eligibility ----
\subsection{Eligibility Check}
\shot{07-eligibility}{The eligibility check asks one plain question at a time, with
an optional explanation of why we are asking, and a progress bar.}
\shot{08-eligibility-result}{The result screen gives a clear verdict. Even when the
user may not qualify, it preserves their freedom to continue or to look for a more
suitable service.}
\textbf{Purpose.} Save wasted effort by checking suitability early, without
discouraging the user.
\begin{itemize}
  \prin{Error prevention (N5, S5)}{Stop problems early}{Checking eligibility before
    the form prevents the larger failure of filling in everything and then being
    rejected.}
  \prin{Help and documentation (N10)}{Explain the question}{Each question offers a
    why are we asking this note, which builds understanding and trust.}
  \prin{Visibility of system status (N1, D3)}{Map progress clearly}{A question one
    of two progress bar maps directly to how far the user has come.}
  \prin{User control and freedom (N3, S7)}{Respect the user's choice}{An
    ineligible result never blocks the user. They can continue anyway and let an
    officer decide, or ask for help finding a different service.}
  \prin{Informative feedback (S3)}{Announce the outcome}{The result sits in a live
    region, so a screen reader announces the verdict right away.}
\end{itemize}

% ---- Documents ----
\subsection{Document Upload}
\shot{09-documents}{The document step. Each requirement is its own card with the
accepted formats, a size limit, examples of acceptable documents, and upload or
take photo actions.}
\textbf{Purpose.} Make uploading the right documents easy and free of errors.
\begin{itemize}
  \prin{Recognition rather than recall (N6)}{Show the acceptable options}{A what
    can I use note lists concrete examples such as Aadhaar or Voter ID, so the user
    does not have to remember what qualifies.}
  \prin{Error prevention (N5, S5)}{Check before accepting}{Only PDF, JPG, or PNG
    files within the size limit are accepted, and invalid files are rejected with a
    clear reason before anything is submitted.}
  \prin{Visibility of system status (N1, S3)}{Confirm success}{An uploaded document
    turns green with a tick and shows its file name and size, so the user can see
    it was accepted.}
  \prin{Flexibility and efficiency (N7)}{Fit the device}{Both an upload option and
    a take photo option are offered, and the second one is ideal on a phone.}
  \prin{Constraints (D4)}{Guide valid input}{The file picker is limited to the
    accepted file types, which nudges the user toward a valid choice.}
\end{itemize}

% ---- Form ----
\subsection{Application Form}
\shot{10-form}{The form presents one small step at a time, with plain labels,
helper text, examples, and inline checking. It never shows a wall of thirty
fields.}
\textbf{Purpose.} Collect accurate information with the least possible mental
effort.
\begin{itemize}
  \prin{Reduce short term memory load (S8, N6)}{Break the work into pieces}{The form
    is split into small themed steps for personal details, contact, address, and
    income, instead of one overwhelming page.}
  \prin{Error prevention (N5, S5)}{Catch mistakes as they happen}{Fields are checked
    as the user types, for example a twelve digit Aadhaar, a ten digit mobile
    number, or a six digit PIN code, each with a specific and friendly message.}
  \prin{Match with the real world (N2)}{Use human labels}{Labels and helper text
    use everyday phrasing, such as estimated annual income of the household, with
    worked examples.}
  \prin{Visibility of system status (N1, D3)}{Show progress}{A step indicator shows
    how many steps are left, which maps effort to completion.}
  \prin{User control and freedom (N3, S6)}{Never lose work}{A save and finish later
    option stores a draft at any point, so users can leave and return safely.}
\end{itemize}

  \prin{Design dialogs to yield closure (S4)}{Mark a clear end}{The review gives a

% ====================== CROSS-CUTTING ======================
\section{Cross Cutting Principles}
Some principles are not tied to a single screen but run through the whole product.
\begin{itemize}
  \prin{Help users recover from errors (N9)}{Give plain and constructive errors}
    {Error messages everywhere are written in human language and suggest a next
    step, for example please enter a valid ten digit mobile number, or a code error
    that clears the field and invites another try. Network problems fall back
    gracefully so the user is never stuck.}
  \prin{Keep users in control (S7, N3)}{Stay predictable}{Actions are started by the
    user. Anything that cannot be undone, such as a final submit, needs an explicit
    confirmation, and can be approached through a reversible draft first.}
  \prin{Consistency (N4, S1, D5)}{Use one design language}{Shared components and
    tokens make every button, card, badge, and field behave the same way across the
    app.}
  \prin{Accessibility and POUR}{Be inclusive by default}{Keyboard operation with
    visible focus, a skip link, ARIA roles and labels, scalable text, a high
    contrast theme, and reduced motion support make the app usable by a far wider
    range of people.}
  \prin{Conceptual model (D6)}{Give a clear mental picture}{The same five stage
    journey of discover, check, prepare, fill, and confirm gives users an accurate
    idea of how applying works, and it carries across all nine services.}
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
EasySeva shows that the difference between an intimidating government portal and one
that actually helps people comes down, for the most part, to applied Human Computer
Interaction. By grounding every screen in ideas that are well established, such as
keeping status visible, speaking the user's language, preventing errors before they
happen, favouring recognition over memory, keeping the user in control, and
designing for everyone, the application turns a rare and stressful task into one
that feels calm, guided, and forgiving.

These principles are not decoration. Each one maps to a real reduction in the
failures that citizens face, including abandoned forms, rejected documents, lost
progress, and confusion about status. Good design, in other words, is what lets
technology truly serve people. EasySeva is a working and deployed example of that
idea.
